Not to scale: distances are compressed (∝ AU0.55) and planet sizes exaggerated (∝ √diameter) so everything is visible. Orbital periods are correctly proportional; self-rotation is slowed 50× for visibility. The Moon is shown ~2× too big and its orbit hugely enlarged (really ~30 Earth-diameters away) so it's visible. Orbits are drawn as circles in one plane; planet positions are approximate mean longitudes for the shown date.
Galaxy view: scale is wildly compressed. 1 grid unit ≈ 100 light-years. At this scale the whole Solar System (out to Neptune, ~0.0005 light-years across) would be far smaller than a single pixel, so the Sun marker is hugely exaggerated. Particles stand in for billions of stars; the galaxy's rotation is sped up by trillions of times. Zoom in, press Esc or tap ☀ Solar System to go back.
