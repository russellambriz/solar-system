Not to scale: distances are compressed (∝ AU0.55) and planet sizes exaggerated (∝ √diameter) so everything is visible. Orbital periods are correctly proportional; self-rotation is slowed 50× for visibility. The Moon is shown ~2× too big and its orbit hugely enlarged (really ~30 Earth-diameters away) so it's visible. Moons of Mars and Jupiter are likewise enlarged, and their orbits are slowed (Jupiter's 20×, Mars' 60×) so they don't blur; their order and relative periods are real. Moons of Saturn, Uranus and Neptune are enlarged the same way, slowed 30×, and their distances compressed (logarithmically) so each system fits; their order, relative periods and tilts are real (Triton orbits backwards). Planet orbits are drawn as circles in one plane; planet positions are approximate mean longitudes for the shown date. Dwarf planets follow their real tilted, eccentric orbits (same distance compression), but asteroids and Kuiper-belt objects are drawn as huge glowing dots: the real belts are mostly empty space. Charon's orbit is shrunk and slowed 20×. Spacecraft are drawn thousands of times too big; the ISS flies ~3.5× too high (1,500 km) and its orbit is slowed ~300×, and JWST is drawn just past the Moon's orbit (really ~4× farther).
Local Group: true scale. Andromeda is shown at its real distance, ~2.5 million light-years away (about 25 Milky Way diameters), and both galaxies are drawn to scale. Andromeda is ~220,000 ly across, about twice the Milky Way. The dwarf companions M32 and M110 are shown in approximate positions. Tap 🌌 Milky Way or press Esc to return. The Sombrero Galaxy (M104) is also at its true distance, ~31 million light-years (12× farther than Andromeda), and to scale (~50,000 ly across); far away it is marked with a glow so you can find it. TON 618, a quasar powered by one of the most massive black holes known, is shown at its light-travel distance of ~10.8 billion light-years (~350× farther than the Sombrero); its black-hole core is enlarged ~16,000× so you can see it. Galaxy view: scale is wildly compressed. 1 grid unit ≈ 100 light-years. At this scale the whole Solar System (out to Neptune, ~0.0005 light-years across) would be far smaller than a single pixel, so the Sun marker is hugely exaggerated. Particles stand in for billions of stars; the galaxy's rotation is sped up by trillions of times. Zoom in, press Esc or tap ☀ Solar System to go back. Nebulae and star clusters are at their real positions relative to the Sun, but drawn far larger than life so they're visible. Zoom far out (or tap 🔭 Andromeda) to see our neighbor galaxy.
